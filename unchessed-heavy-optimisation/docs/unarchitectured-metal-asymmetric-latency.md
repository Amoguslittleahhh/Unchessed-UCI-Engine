# Unarchitectured Metal asymmetric opponent-latency evaluation

## Scope and provenance

This evaluation is part of the **Unarchitectured Metal** series. The Metal runtime artifact used in every game was `artifacts/unarchitectured-metal-final.unmetal`. The experiment driver is `tools/run_unarchitectured_metal_asymmetric_latency.py`; analysis is generated by `tools/analyse_unarchitectured_metal_asymmetric_latency.py` and `tools/make_unarchitectured_metal_latency_report.py`.

The opponent was Stockfish 16 from the Ubuntu package, driven through UCI. Unchessed was configured with one thread, 64 MiB hash, adaptive mode, no own book, adapter telemetry enabled, and `UCI_Opponent=- - human UnknownOpponent`. The unknown declaration prevents the known-engine table from deciding the result. Each game used one of six fixed opening prefixes and was observed for up to 40 plies. The experiment therefore used live engine interaction and live detector telemetry; it did not replay or simulate observations.

## Arms and results

The standard arm used `AcceleratedDetection=false`. The fusion arm used `AcceleratedDetection=true`, with the same build and Metal artifact. The summary below is generated from the committed `results/unarchitectured-metal-latency-report/latency_summary.json` artifact.

| Condition | Arm | Games | Full rate | Mean first-Full ply | Median | Fusion-trigger rate |
|---|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 80 ms/move | Standard | 10 | 100% | 30.0 | 30.0 | 0% |
| 80 ms/move | Fusion | 10 | 100% | 30.6 | 31.0 | 40% |
| 30 ms/move | Standard | 6 | 100% | 30.0 | 31.0 | 0% |
| 30 ms/move | Fusion | 6 | 100% | 29.67 | 31.0 | 50% |

At 80 ms, fusion was earlier in 2 of 10 fixed-prefix pairs, equal in 4, and later in 4, for a mean paired difference of +0.6 plies. At 30 ms, fusion was earlier in 2 of 6 pairs, equal in 3, and later in 1, for a mean paired difference of -0.33 plies. These are descriptive paired comparisons, not causal estimates, because the earlier mode transition can alter later Unchessed moves and consequently the evidence stream.

Across all 32 games, the adapter produced no illegal moves, crashes, or process failures. Fusion triggered its new reason in 7 of 16 enabled games; the other enabled games used the unchanged legacy ceiling. The result demonstrates real asymmetric operation and occasional earlier confirmation, but it does not establish uniformly lower latency.

## Calibration

The first real Stockfish telemetry run showed that the initial fusion accumulator was too conservative: its negative-evidence center was too high for the observed convergence path. The calibrated constants are a score threshold of 0.35, leaky decay of 0.96, negative-evidence center of 0.20, evidence floor of 0.28, gain of 4.0, and a two-observation agreement streak. The harmonic mean across rating, confidence, stability, and trend remains the central false-positive safeguard. Fusion is evaluated before the legacy ceiling only when the default-off option is enabled; standard detection remains unchanged.

## Validation status and limitations

The release adapter builds successfully. The complete `unchessed-core` library suite passes 128 tests, including 23 opponent-model tests; the telemetry parser suite passes five tests, including backward compatibility for older schema-v1 records. The IEEEtran paper and figure are in `ieee-paper/accelerated_detection_asymmetric_latency.tex` and `ieee-paper/accelerated_detection_asymmetric_latency.pdf`.

The current evidence is not flawless. Only Stockfish 16 was used as the distinct opponent, the game horizon was 40 plies, and the fixed opening set is small. The next test series should add a weaker engine, a human-like policy, and real human games, then measure false positives as well as time-to-Full. Results should be reported with confidence intervals and a preregistered stopping rule rather than promoted from these runs as a universal threshold.
